## Problem and context

What problem, opportunity or institutional condition requires advocacy? What evidence establishes the context?

## Objective

What specific change is sought? Who has authority to make or implement it? What would observable success look like?

## Stakeholders and power

Identify decision-makers, affected communities, allies, opponents, implementers, experts and relevant networks. Link to evidence-backed stakeholder mapping where available.

## Theory of change

Describe the hypothesised pathway from advocacy activity to desired outcomes. State assumptions and uncertainty explicitly.

## Narrative and messages

What propositions must be communicated, to whom, through which credible messengers, and with what evidence?

## Engagement and tactics

For each proposed action record target, purpose, owner, timing, dependencies, evidence requirement and approval requirement.

## Evidence and confidence

List material claims, supporting evidence, provenance, confidence and known contradictions or gaps.

## Risks and safeguards

Consider affected communities, representation, privacy, safety, legal/compliance requirements, reputational effects and unintended consequences.

## Measures

Define outputs, intermediate outcomes, outcome indicators and learning signals. Avoid treating activity volume alone as evidence of impact.

## Review and adaptation

Define review triggers, decision rights and the conditions under which the strategy should continue, change, pause or stop.

## Approval

Canonical approval is a human decision. Record approver, decision date, conditions and decision record reference.
