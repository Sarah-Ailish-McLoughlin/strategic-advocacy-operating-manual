# Advocacy Theory of Change

## Purpose

Make explicit how proposed advocacy activity is expected to contribute to a desired change, what must be true for that pathway to work, and what evidence would support or challenge the strategy.

## Method

1. State the desired outcome in observable terms.
2. Identify the decisions, behaviours, institutional conditions or implementation changes required to reach it.
3. Work backwards to identify intermediate outcomes.
4. Identify actors and mechanisms that could produce those outcomes.
5. Record assumptions connecting activity to output, output to outcome, and outcome to impact.
6. Attach evidence and confidence to material assumptions.
7. Identify plausible alternative explanations, risks and unintended effects.
8. Define indicators that would show whether the pathway is strengthening, weakening or failing.
9. Establish review points at which the strategy may be adapted, stopped or escalated for decision.

## Minimum output

The theory of change should contain the advocacy objective, intended outcomes, intermediate outcomes, activities, mechanisms, assumptions, evidence, confidence, indicators, risks and review triggers.

## Decision rule

A theory of change is a testable strategic hypothesis, not proof that an intervention will cause an outcome. Material uncertainty must remain visible.
