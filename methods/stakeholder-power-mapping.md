# Stakeholder and Power Mapping

## Purpose

Identify the people, institutions, networks, constituencies and other actors that can affect an advocacy objective, and make the assumptions behind engagement choices explicit.

## Inputs

- advocacy objective
- available evidence
- relevant jurisdictions and decision processes
- known stakeholders and affected communities
- constraints, risks and uncertainties

## Method

1. Define the decision or change being sought.
2. Identify actors who hold formal authority, informal influence, expertise, resources, legitimacy, implementation power, or lived experience relevant to that change.
3. Record evidence for each assessment rather than treating influence or position as fact without provenance.
4. Assess current position toward the objective, degree of influence, degree of impact from the issue, relationships, and uncertainty.
5. Identify missing voices and representation risks.
6. Select engagement approaches proportionate to evidence, authority, risk and context.
7. Review the map when material evidence, relationships or institutional conditions change.

## Minimum output

A stakeholder record should capture an identifier, actor or group, role, relationship to the objective, position, influence, impact, evidence, confidence, engagement approach, owner and review date.

## Safeguards

Stakeholder mapping must not become a covert profiling mechanism. Record only information necessary for legitimate advocacy planning. Distinguish evidence from inference, avoid sensitive personal data unless there is a justified and governed need, and provide human review for consequential engagement decisions.

## Machine maturity

This method is currently human-readable. Structured stakeholder records and validation schemas are proposed Level 2 artefacts and require review before canonical adoption.
