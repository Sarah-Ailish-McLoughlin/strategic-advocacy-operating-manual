# Purpose

The Strategic Advocacy Operating Manual exists to make strategic advocacy more deliberate, evidence-aware, reusable, governable, and capable of progressive machine assistance.

It provides a common operating frame for moving from an advocacy objective to a governed programme of analysis, strategy, engagement, action, learning, and adaptation.

The manual is intended to support practitioners and organisations without assuming that advocacy is a single campaign technique. It treats advocacy as an operating capability whose decisions, evidence, methods, outputs, and learning can be made explicit.

## Human purpose

The human purpose of this manual is to strengthen the ability of people, communities, advocates, and organisations to pursue fair and accountable change when individual action alone is insufficient to influence the systems that affect people's lives.

Strategic advocacy can help close the gap between rights or legitimate human needs recognised in principle and people's ability to realise them in practice. The manual therefore supports work that helps people understand systems, exercise agency, participate meaningfully in decisions that affect them, preserve and communicate lived experience and evidence, and hold power and institutions accountable.

A useful human-centred pathway is:

**human need, right, or legitimate interest → lived experience and evidence → understanding the system and its power relationships → defining the change sought → proportionate strategy and engagement → accountable decisions and action → outcomes → learning and systemic improvement**

The manual does not assume that advocacy success is automatically a human benefit. A successful campaign, engagement, or institutional decision can still exclude affected people, redistribute harms, or reinforce unequal power. Advocacy should therefore remain attentive to questions such as who benefits, who bears risk, who participated, whose voice is absent, whether participation was accessible, and whether affected people retained meaningful agency.

## Human rights and public benefit

This manual can support the advancement of human rights by making rights-oriented advocacy more understandable, evidence-informed, accessible, accountable, and reusable. It is intended to help advocates move from a recognised need or right toward practical systemic change without treating technology, evidence, or institutional process as substitutes for human agency.

The manual does not itself determine which contested claim is correct, declare that a particular outcome advances human rights, or grant authority to act on another person's behalf. Rights claims, legal interpretations, ethical judgements, advocacy priorities, and consequential decisions require appropriate human judgement, participation, evidence, and—where relevant—professional or legal expertise.

Its normative direction is therefore:

> **Technology assists understanding. Evidence supports claims. Structure improves accountability. People retain agency and authority.**

Machine validation establishes only that specified structural or semantic requirements have been satisfied. It must not be interpreted as evidence that an advocacy position is ethically justified, legally correct, beneficial to affected people, approved for publication, or endorsed by a person or community.

## Foundation objectives

- provide clear human-readable guidance;
- define reusable strategic advocacy concepts and practices;
- strengthen meaningful participation, agency, accessibility, and accountability in advocacy practice;
- preserve lived experience, evidence, and decision provenance without treating any single evidence source as automatically authoritative;
- make power relationships, assumptions, decisions, and intended outcomes more explicit where practicable;
- introduce structured machine-readable representations;
- enable deterministic validation and later authorised assistance without prematurely granting machine authority;
- preserve human authority over consequential advocacy, publication, engagement, and canonical decisions.

The ultimate purpose of increasing the manual's technical maturity is not automation for its own sake. It is to make strategic advocacy knowledge easier to understand, scrutinise, reuse, improve, and apply in ways that can help people participate in decisions affecting them and pursue fairer systemic outcomes.
